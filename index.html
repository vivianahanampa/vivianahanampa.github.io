About

the short version

I'm a data scientist and researcher finishing an M.S. in Data Science at Columbia University. At the Zuckerman Institute I work on neural encoding models — predicting fMRI brain activity from vision-language model representations, and asking whether multimodal networks encode anything beyond what vision and language encode separately.

Before Columbia I spent three years shipping machine learning in Lima, Peru. At Interbank I built credit-risk models serving 50,000+ customers and a retrieval-augmented generation system over financial filings. At Telefónica I deployed clustering models that cut field response times by 85%. At IBM I architected a real-time fraud-detection pipeline adopted by seven enterprise clients.

I trained as an electronic engineer first, which is probably why I like problems that run all the way from raw signal to deployed system. Research interests: neural encoding and decoding, multimodal representations across vision, language and audio, brain–computer interfaces, and applied machine learning for health.

Projects

questions, not demos

Algonauts 2025 — vision-language encoding

In progress

Do vision–language embeddings predict visual cortex better than vision-only embeddings — and if so, where?

Encoding models over 1,000 cortical regions comparing CLIP and SigLIP against DINOv2 and supervised ViT at matched architecture, on public fMRI data. The prediction, stated in advance: the multimodal advantage should be near zero in V1 and grow through the ventral stream.

So farAcross all 1,000 regions, the self-supervised vision model predicts brain activity 31% better than the language-supervised one.

EEG-to-text decoding

Prototype

Can an 8-channel EEG headset recover the words someone is reading or hearing — and can the brain's own surprise signal catch the decoder's mistakes?

Contrastive learning maps 8-channel EEG into T5 word-embedding space, then retrieves candidate words for each position. N400 features — the brain's response to a semantically unexpected word — flag implausible candidates, and a language model reconstructs the sentence from what's left. Built with Hazel Lim, on the open codebase from Decoding individual words from non-invasive brain recordings.

Deep RL for competitive Pokémon

Complete

Can an agent learn competitive play well enough to consistently beat scripted opponents?

A Rainbow-style DQN — dueling heads, noisy-net exploration, prioritized replay with 3-step returns — trained over 2,000 Gen 1 battles on a local Pokémon Showdown server through UT Austin's metamon environment. 150+ engineered state features with reward shaping for long-horizon planning, and a 100K-transition replay buffer that cut training time 60%.

ResultIt wins up to 70% of evaluation battles against a random opponent, but no more than 15% against the scripted Gen 1 boss AI. Consistently beating scripted play is still open.

edgar-rag

In progress

Does chunking strategy actually change answer accuracy in financial-document RAG — or do we just assume it does?

Three retrieval configurations over SEC filings, one fixed generator, one labelled question set — including deliberately unanswerable questions, because knowing when to abstain is the part most RAG evaluations skip. Scored on recall@k, MRR, faithfulness and cost per query.
