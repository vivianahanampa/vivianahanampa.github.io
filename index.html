Vision-language models and the human brain

Researcher, NeuroTech Columbia, Mesgarani Lab, Jan 2026 – present

Does training a model with language make it more brain-like? I fit encoding models that predict fMRI responses in 1,000 cortical regions of 4 people watching Friends, using DINOv3, SigLIP2 and Qwen3 features, and test them on a held-out season. So far vision wins: a self-supervised vision model predicts the brain 31% better than a language-supervised one in all 4 participants.

PyTorch, ridge encoding models, multimodal embeddingsCode in a private repository

Read moreShow less

The question

DINOv3 learns from images alone, SigLIP2 learns from images paired with text, and Qwen3 is a language model. If training with language makes a model's representations more like the brain's, SigLIP2 should predict visual brain activity better than DINOv3. I test that on fMRI recorded while people watched Friends, from the Algonauts 2025 data.

How I built it

I extract features from each model for the video and fit ridge encoding models that map them to activity in 1,000 cortical regions for each of the 4 participants. The models train on seasons 1 to 5 and are tested on season 6, which they never see. A good part of the work was processing and aligning the fMRI data across participants, and benchmarking multimodal models against unimodal ones.

What I've found so far

The self-supervised vision model wins in all 4 participants. Adding language features on top of vision still helps, by another 18% (mean r = 0.18, with non-overlapping 95% confidence intervals). Linyang He supervises the project, and Prof. Nima Mesgarani is the faculty advisor.

Compact sign language recognition for low-resource languages

Research Collaborator, Marist University (remote), 2026

Peruvian and Argentinian Sign Language have very little training data, so I designed a small model for them that recognizes signs from body keypoints and needs no pretraining. It performs as well as a pretrained system hundreds of times larger and runs in milliseconds, even inside a web browser.

PyTorch, pose transformer, ONNX, WebAssembly

Read moreShow less

The question

Can a sign language model small enough to run on an ordinary laptop, or in a browser, keep up with a large pretrained model?

How I built it

The model reads body-movement keypoints instead of raw video, which keeps the input small. I trained a compact pose transformer in PyTorch with no pretraining, including a step that shows which body parts the model relies on for each sign. Then I exported it with ONNX and WebAssembly so it runs in the browser.

What worked

It matches the pretrained model while being hundreds of times smaller, and it beats the other non-pretrained models it was compared with. The body-part step makes its decisions easier to interpret, too.

Pneumoconiosis diagnosis from chest X-rays

First author, PUCP Medical Imaging Laboratory, 2022–2024. IEEE EMBC 2024.

I led a 5-person team that built a diagnostic tool for pneumoconiosis, an occupational lung disease common among miners. A U-Net outlines the lungs (96% Dice), then a ResNet-50 classifies the disease (87% accuracy).

PyTorch, TensorFlow/Keras, U-Net, ResNet-50Paper

Read moreShow less

The problem

Pneumoconiosis is a rare disease, so there are few labeled chest X-rays to learn from. We wanted a diagnostic tool that stays reliable anyway.

How we built it

I led a 5-person interdisciplinary team, supervised by Dr. Stefano Romero. We split the task in two: a U-Net outlines the lungs, then a ResNet-50 classifies the disease. To get the most out of 1,200+ annotated images, we used transfer learning across 5 models and added data augmentation.

What worked

The lung outlines overlap the experts' at 96% (Dice), and the classifier reaches 87% accuracy. The work became my first-author paper at IEEE EMBC 2024 in Orlando.

EEG-to-text decoding

Team project with Hazel Lim, Columbia, 2026

Can an 8-channel EEG headset recover the words someone is reading? And can the brain's own surprise signal catch the decoder's mistakes?

PyTorch, contrastive learning, EEGCode

Read moreShow less

How we built it

We use contrastive learning to map EEG into T5 word-embedding space and retrieve candidate words for each position in the sentence. The N400, the brain's surprise response to an unexpected word, flags the implausible candidates, and a language model rebuilds the sentence from what's left. We built on the open codebase from Decoding individual words from non-invasive brain recordings.

LLM retrieval over 500+ earnings reports

Data Scientist, Interbank, 2024–2025

A retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system that pulls financial metrics out of earnings reports and turns them into structured data. It agreed with analysts' manual extraction 92% of the time, and fine-tuning the embeddings raised retrieval precision 25%.

OpenAI API, LangChain, Pinecone, PyTorch

Read moreShow less

The problem

Analysts at Interbank pulled financial metrics out of earnings reports by hand. I built a system to do that extraction across 500+ reports.

How I built it

The reports are indexed in Pinecone, LangChain retrieves the relevant passages, and an LLM through the OpenAI API extracts the metrics into a structured format. I fine-tuned the embedding model in PyTorch so retrieval finds the right passages more often, which is where the 25% gain in precision came from.

k-means, XGBoost, PySpark, DistilBERT, FastAPI, Power BI

The problem

Three parts of field operations in Lima: how the city is divided into technician service zones, which of the 500+ daily repair requests get handled first, and what customers say in their reviews.

How I built it

I used k-means to redraw Lima into 3 service zones, with a Power BI dashboard on top. For repair requests, I built an XGBoost model that ranks tickets and helps route technicians, and we rolled it out with 3 contractors per zone. I also fine-tuned DistilBERT on 10,000+ reviews (89% accuracy) and served it as a real-time FastAPI microservice. Underneath all of it, I cleaned up 10+ years of legacy records (2M+ rows) with PySpark, which also unblocked 3 production ML pipelines.
